# Microslop Copilot and AI Hype

I'll admit it, I was one of the testers on the _GitHub Copilot_ technical preview
back in 2022. I thought it was so good, the autocomplete was amazing and I
never thought this "AI" programmer side-kick could be so good. I thought it was
just something like cleverbot for programming.

When the technical preview finished in August, I felt a little lost, I realised
I got really used to this _Copilot_ doing a lot of the repetitive tasks that a
programmer needs to do (specially in a language like PHP). I wanted to justify
paying for the _Copilot_'s 100$/yr subscription even when I was already having a
hard time with the price of everything else going up!

![Screenshot from copilot at the time](/public/images/the-microslop-hype/the-microslop-hype_screenie-1.webp)

At the time, there wasn't a lot of available options for me that looked good
and I was afraid they didn't work as well as Copilot. So after a couple of
months, I caved...

## AI-brained

The incredible part about early AI assistance is that nobody knew we were
training it by using it (at least I didn't lol), and another part was that it
kinda exceeded every expectation I had of an autocomplete while just being a
fancy autocomplete.

While I was back to trying it out again, I was working on an integration with
a few sales platforms for my j*b to retrieve order data and other sorts of
data. This is when I realised: "I don't have to look up any of the API
documentation, I can just use the autocomplete to guess the URLs for requests
better than me!". This realisation lead me to just ignore documentation others
wrote in favour of "moving fast, breaking things" which at the time was ok
since my coworkers and I loved "testing in production" :P.

It was kind of amazing, but as it got better, I got worse, it was like a
dementor sucking the programming mastery out of me. I was so attached to this
assistant for helping me with annoying tasks that I forgot how to look up
certain information. The _StackOverflow_ that had helped me throughout the
years, the developer documentation, I had forgotten how to search for and
through those. I only understood what I had lost when I started programming on
my bus commute every morning, I decided to learn _Rust_ on my free time and
I didn't have internet on the bus. This _Copilot_ was no-longer there to guide
my hand and all I had was the
[_Rust Cookbook_](https://doc.rust-lang.org/book/) downloaded.

## Rehabilitation

I almost gave up learning _Rust_ on the bus, I was thinking "I'll just do it at
home on my PC with Copilot", knowing full well that I would just use that free
time to play video games or watch movies. The problem was that I didn't "feel
like" learning _Rust_ but I really wanted to (I had heard it was a really good
language, don't judge me ok?).

I also had started having problems with my wrist, I was getting some sort of
pain in my carpal tunnel and was looking for a solution. I bought a vertical
mouse, a standing desk and that helped but I was still hurting myself on the
bus, always moving my hand to the mousepad in such a cramped space sitting on
the bus. I asked some friends about it and they recommended I try a keyboard
only text-editor, _vim_. So instead of learning _Rust_, I was booting up
_vim :Tutor_ on the bus to learn the sorcery of "vim keybinds".

![my work desk](/public/images/my-work-desk-1.webp)

_I didn't have a old picture of my laptop on the bus so here's a picture of
my work desk_

This learning process of trying something new without using _Copilot_ reminded
me of what it was like to program without an assistant "helping" me with my
code. I started to use _vim_ to program in _Rust_ and at the time, I didn't
know if I could install _Copilot_ onto _vim_ but it also never occured to me.

At work I kept using _Copilot_ for PHP, Laravel and some Javascript but I was
slowly using it less and less. I was working off of the documentation for
libraries I was using, reading through code, writing more code and I felt
like I started to like my j*b again (I know, incredible).

Then the day came where I got the email telling me my _Copilot_ subscription
would be auto-renewed... and I canceled it.

## Back to the topic

Ever since this rehab, I've been pretty anti-agent, I know that it would take
away my passion for programming and it probably takes it away from all of us.
But what inspired me to make this post was a recent post I saw from someone
else:
[How many products does Microsoft have named 'Copilot'? I mapped every one](https://teybannerman.com/strategy/2026/03/31/how-many-microsoft-copilot-are-there.html).
This post, showed me in one image how far we've come from the humble assitant.

![Copilot is in every microslop program](/public/images/the-microslop-hype/the-microslop-hype_screenie-2.webp)

The fact Microslop is putting Copilot into the name of **LAPTOPS**, in
**KEYBOARDS** and in the **NOTEPAD** should show you how far Microslop is
willing to push their _Copilot_ on us. Cmon, who is asking for
[_Gaming Copilot_](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2025/09/18/gaming-copilot-xbox-pc-mobile/)?

I think the problem is that these "AI" companies are trying to pay for the hole
they have created so they are trying to put these features into everything,
even if the overall products get worse.

![The GitHub missing status page, showing that github has had a less than 90% overall uptime of all its features](/public/images/the-microslop-hype/the-microslop-hype_screenie-3.webp)

It's not like anyone is really asking for this besides the share-holders right?
And I guess the ~crypto-bros~ ai-bros? But if it is the share-holders, are they
not seeing the graphs? The debt? The critics? Or are they just holding until it
hits the moon? I don't understand how these "AI-powered" features are being
added to every part of our digital lives. Even if some of them are good, I
don't think the gamble on every program is worth the bloat.

Personally, I actually think the in-line completion of many of the current
assistants is pretty good but nowadays you can't get that anymore without the
"agentic" features.

I'm just a little disapointed, I've been programming for a while and the most
annoying bubble used to be the crypto/web3 hype but it doesn't compare with
this "AI" push. It's not even actual "Artificial Inteligence", it's just a
probability machine around a Large Language Model.

## What I really don't understand

I go to a couple of programming meetups here and there and also hang out in
some internet circles and throughout the last few months some of my friends
have started using these LLMs to write notes for them... WHY????? What could
possibly be the advantage of letting the agent take them for you? I know,
I know, it's really good for dictation, letting one of these programs listen to
a meeting and taking those notes for you and summarising them at the end is
really handy, I can understand the need for that. But some people tell an agent
to look up things, read about those things, take notes about those things and
summarize them into a small markdown file all on their own without any human
intervention. Sounds great, it's like a personal assistant, except that you, as
a human, never read the original work. "AI" agents are
[known to hallucinate](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/ai-hallucinations),
[even after you try to reduce it](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/382629296_Reducing_hallucination_in_structured_outputs_via_Retrieval-Augmented_Generation).
How can you trust these notes, and if you didn't write them, how are you going
to remember the notes?

![classic google search result, showing that to make cheese stick to a pizza, you should add glue](/public/images/the-microslop-hype/the-microslop-hype_figure-1.webp)

## The price

I also want to talk about one thing that I don't see enough people discussing:
the price. Honestly, it started pretty cheap, 10$/month for an assistant that
can boost your productivity by a lot is a really good price, but the incessant
need to be more productive, having more agents running simultaneously, spending
more tokens is kinda strange to me. I normally try to be pretty frugal, just
trying to only spend enough on what I need and try not to waste other people's
resources, even if that means not getting the best of the best hosting server,
I try to get something that is "just enough to work pretty well". Seeing these
people "burn through" thousands of tokens a day just doesn't sit right with me.
These prices are only going to increase, like how _Netflix_ was really cheap
initially but now the price is almost unjustifiable for me.

![a post from reddit, someone complaining that they just purchased a subscription to claude code and already have used 37% of tokens in 20 minutes](/public/images/the-microslop-hype/the-microslop-hype_figure-2.webp)

Alright, I think I've rambled on for long enough. I don't know where I was
going with this article but I guess so didn't you. I hope you can understand
my point of view or maybe even agree with me on some of these points. In an
age of slop, how can we stay unsloppy?
